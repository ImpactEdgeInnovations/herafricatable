# Her Africa Table — Community Product Vision

## Product thesis

Her Africa Table is a trust-gated community network for African women. Events
establish the first layer of trust; the platform turns that first meeting into
an enduring community, relevant relationships, shared opportunity, and
measurable outcomes.

Community is the product's retention layer, not an optional social feed:

`event or referral → approved member → home community → introduction → Ask or Offer → relationship → outcome → trusted referral`

## The five community pillars

1. **Conversation** — guided introductions, focused discussions, Asks, Offers,
   resources, and outcomes.
2. **Connection** — trusted profiles, curated introductions, mutual consent,
   and private relationships.
3. **Gathering** — events, dinners, Circles, and deliberate continuation after
   each table.
4. **Growth** — learning, opportunities, practical support, and visible
   progress between events.
5. **Stewardship** — approved hosts, shared standards, moderation, privacy, and
   community-health oversight.

## Community operating model

The platform must build density before breadth:

1. **The Founding Table** — a private Nairobi cohort linked to the first event,
   with consent-based invitations, guided introductions, and follow-up.
2. **City Chapters** — official communities opened only when member density,
   an approved host, moderation coverage, and a reliable gathering rhythm exist.
3. **Curated Circles** — small, time-bound groups matched around a useful goal
   and reviewed by a human before publication.
4. **Interest Communities** — industry or identity spaces introduced in
   response to demonstrated member demand.
5. **Member-led Communities** — approved hosts operating under Her Africa Table
   standards after host onboarding, support, analytics, and offboarding controls
   pass production acceptance.

These are stages, not simultaneous launch promises.

## Member experience

Every active member should be able to understand her community home without
training. A community contains five simple areas:

- **Overview** — purpose, host, next gathering, current prompt, and one next action.
- **Conversations** — structured introductions, Asks, Offers, opportunities,
  resources, wins, and announcements.
- **Members** — community-specific discovery with shared context and
  consent-based connection actions.
- **Gatherings** — events, Circle sessions, and post-event continuation.
- **Resources** — host-approved guides, recordings, learning, and opportunities.

The initial conversation categories are:

- Start Here
- Introductions
- Asks
- Offers
- Opportunities
- Resources
- Events and Follow-up
- Wins and Outcomes
- Announcements

## Trust boundaries

- Platform approval and community admission are separate decisions.
- Private spaces require invitation or host approval.
- A member does not enter an event-linked room until she accepts.
- Private messaging requires mutual connection consent.
- Bilateral blocks apply across discovery, feeds, introductions, and messaging.
- Moderators receive only report-scoped evidence; they cannot browse private
  conversations without cause.
- Host, moderator, and Admin actions are attributable and auditable.
- Feature flags remain database-enforced release controls.

## Recognition, not popularity

Her Africa Table will not copy public points leaderboards at launch. Recognition
should reward meaningful contribution rather than posting volume:

- Founding Member
- Thoughtful Contributor
- Connector
- Event Steward
- Opportunity Maker
- Community Host

Recognition must be connected to useful actions or reviewed outcomes and must
not expose private relationship notes.

## Success measures

Registration volume alone does not prove a community. The operating dashboard
should measure:

- invitation acceptance;
- guided-introduction completion;
- members forming at least two mutual connections;
- weekly and monthly participating members;
- Ask and Offer response rate;
- gathering attendance and post-event continuation;
- 30- and 90-day retention;
- voluntary, privacy-safe member outcomes;
- unanswered requests and members at risk of disengaging;
- moderation and support response time.

An “active member” must always be reported using a defined behavior and time
window, never as a lifetime registration count.

## Delivery sequence

### Foundation

- Make Community a first-class member destination.
- Give each community a calm overview and clear internal navigation.
- Add structured posts, comments, thoughtful reactions, pinned content, search,
  saving, and thread following.
- Add a privacy-safe community member roster.
- Connect relevant events, Circles, and resources to a community.
- Add community notification preferences and a restrained weekly briefing.

### Host operations

- Add host onboarding, standards acceptance, and role-specific guidance.
- Show pending admissions, incomplete introductions, unanswered Asks, reports,
  upcoming gatherings, participation, retention, and outcomes.
- Add community launch, pause, archive, ownership transfer, and offboarding
  controls.

### Controlled scale

- Validate the Nairobi founding cohort before opening additional chapters.
- Introduce discovery only for approved members and release-ready communities.
- Add paid host tools, tier entitlements, and community commerce only after the
  trust, retention, support, and unit-economics gates pass.

## Current product alignment

The current build already includes the main trust and relationship foundations:
registration and approval, onboarding, profiles, events, attendee and network
discovery, connections, private messaging, Asks and Offers, official/private
communities, event-linked founding cohorts, Circles, learning foundations,
moderation, privacy-safe outcomes, and Admin operations.

The next product task is to bring those capabilities together around Community
as the member's everyday context while keeping incomplete functionality behind
controlled release gates.
